01 / Galaxies

Two trillion islands of light.

Astronomers estimate roughly two trillion galaxies in the observable universe. Each one is a turning disk of hundreds of billions of stars, most of them too faint to see from here.

02 / Nurseries

Stars are born in the cold.

Stars form inside vast clouds of gas and dust. Gravity pulls each cloud inward until the core is hot enough to ignite fusion, and a new sun switches on.

03 / Worlds

The leftovers become worlds.

Planets condense from the disk of material left around a young star. Our own system has eight of them, and most of the rest of the galaxy's worlds are still waiting to be counted.

04 / Cells

Thirty-seven trillion small factories.

A typical adult human body holds an estimated 37 trillion cells. Each is a membrane-bound workshop, and at its center a nucleus holds the instructions it runs on.

05 / Same material

The same stuff all the way down.

Galaxies, clouds, planets and cells are built from the same handful of elements. Most of the carbon and oxygen in your body was forged inside a star that lived and died long before you.
